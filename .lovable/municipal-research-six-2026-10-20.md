# Municipal service research: six cities

**Purpose and format.** Read `src/lib/seo/location-service-drafts.ts`: local plans are short, service-specific planning notes with a heading/body and source title/URL, scoped to CCTV, access, intrusion, remote monitoring and smart-building services. Below are research notes for potential copy, not edits. Facts are distinguished from suggested planning prompts. Public source pages/documents were fetched or checked for public accessibility; linked source content is municipal primary material. Rules may change; confirm current project-specific requirements directly with the relevant authority. No evidence here establishes Fortega service availability.

## Lethbridge

**Supported facts.** City Alarm Systems Bylaw 5078 is listed as in effect on the City's bylaw page; its online consolidation disclaims being the official bylaw text. [Alarm Systems Bylaw (City of Lethbridge)](https://www.lethbridge.ca/media-manager/media-pages/bylaws/alarm-systems-bylaw/) and [consolidated Bylaw 5078 PDF](https://www.lethbridge.ca/media/ldupkmdq/5078-alarms-systems-bylaw.pdf). Lethbridge Fire Prevention’s March 2025 owner reference covers emergency/exit lighting, electromagnetic locks, fire alarms and integrated testing, and says persons installing, inspecting, testing or maintaining life-safety systems must be qualified for the specific system. [Building Owner’s Responsibilities for Fire Safety](https://www.lethbridge.ca/media/badd3tru/lfes-building-owner-responsibilities-for-fire-safety-march-2025.pdf). The City lists response fees for false fire alarms, escalating by annual response count, and identifies dust, hot work and equipment faults among causes. [Fire and Emergency Services](https://www.lethbridge.ca/emergency-services-public-safety/fire-and-emergency-services/).

**Local planning paragraph — intrusion/life safety.** For an alarm or integrated door project, first identify whether the signal is intrusion, fire/life-safety, or both; record monitoring contacts and the agreed test/maintenance process. Lethbridge’s published false-fire-alarm fee guidance makes maintenance and planned work coordination a useful service discussion, not a promise about police response or a claim that a security-alarm permit is needed. For electromagnetic locks or exit-system changes, prepare the existing hardware, exit role, alarm interface and proposed sequence for qualified review against applicable code and City guidance. The City’s owner reference concerns life-safety maintenance; it does not establish that every electronic reader or like-for-like device needs a permit.

**Limitations.** The Alarm Systems Bylaw PDF is explicitly an informational consolidation, not the official version. No city source establishing CCTV rules, a private-building surveillance privacy regime, or a current municipal building-automation rebate was located in this research.

## Calgary

**Supported facts.** Calgary Police/City alarm material describes Alarm Services Bylaw 31M95 and says an alarm permit is required for each alarm system in use at a premises, with permit fees and false-alarm penalties; alarm agencies must be licensed/registered under the stated rules. [Alarm Agency Information Package](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/cps/documents/alarm-bylaw/alarm-agency-information-package.pdf); [Alarm permit application](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/cps/documents/alarm-permit-application.pdf). Calgary Fire Department’s 2023 revised bulletin discusses egress electromagnetic locks, functional annual testing, and release/hardware/delay/control checks. [Fire Marshal Bulletin 2023-001R1: Requirements for Electromagnetic Locks Installed for Security Purposes](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/programs-services/public-and-personal-safety/fire-codes/fire-marshal-bulletins/fire-marshal-bulletin-2023-001-electromagnetic-locks.pdf). The City's Clean Energy Improvement Program permit guide shows requirements vary by measure (e.g., it marks smart thermostats as requiring an electrical permit and some door/window changes as conditional). [Permits for Clean Energy Improvements](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/uep/esm/documents/esm-documents/ceip/permits-for-clean-energy-improvements.pdf).

**Local planning paragraph — intrusion.** Calgary’s alarm-permit materials make it useful to distinguish premises-owner permit responsibility from alarm-agency licensing/registration when scoping an intrusion installation or monitoring change. Establish the system address, permit holder, monitoring contacts and who will handle any application before scheduling; do not imply the installer automatically holds the premises permit or that the published document settles every current exception.

**Local planning paragraph — access/smart controls.** For an electromagnetic lock on an exit route, document existing door hardware, release on fire alarm, any delay, control switch and annual functional-test responsibility before proposing integration. The Fire Marshal bulletin supports these as relevant design/maintenance questions, not a blanket conclusion that a permit is required for all door electronics. For energy-control work, check the specific equipment and scope against the City's CEIP guide and current program eligibility; a smart thermostat is not a general building-automation rebate claim.

**Limitations.** Alarm sources include dated application material; verify current fees and process with CPS. The CEIP guide addresses eligible home energy improvements, not a general commercial BAS incentive. No private-building CCTV privacy rule was established here.

## Edmonton

**Supported facts.** The City’s Alarm Systems Bylaw 10922 states a purpose of reducing false alarms requiring response. [Alarm Systems Bylaw 10922](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/assets/PDF/C10922.pdf). Edmonton Fire Rescue’s unwanted-alarm guidance identifies dust, steam, cooking and renovation-related conditions as causes and offers prevention practices; its annual user checklist says users must notify the City before fire-alarm service such as testing, repair, maintenance, adjustment, alteration or installation. [Preventing Unwanted Alarms](https://www.edmonton.ca/programs_services/preventing-unwanted-alarms); [Annual Alarm System User Checklist](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/AnnualAlarmSystemUserCheckList.pdf?cb=1755189766). Edmonton's Building Energy Benchmarking program offers participating building owners/operators benchmarking reports, technical support and retrofit incentive information, for buildings over 1,000 square feet with owner consent. [Building Energy Benchmarking Program](https://www.edmonton.ca/programs_services/environmental/building-energy-benchmarking-program). Its BERA manual describes rebates for selected efficiency upgrades, including building controls, for commercial/institutional buildings, subject to program eligibility and eligible-measure requirements. [Building Energy Retrofit Accelerator Program Manual](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/documents/PDF/BERA-ProgramManual.pdf).

**Local planning paragraph — alarm.** For Edmonton fire-alarm-connected work, separate an intrusion panel from a fire-alarm system in the initial scope. If fire-alarm servicing is involved, include the City's stated pre-service notification step in the coordination checklist and agree who will notify, test, and manage monitoring. The City's guidance addresses unwanted fire-alarm responses; it does not establish a universal registration or permit requirement for private intrusion alarms.

**Local planning paragraph — automation/energy.** Commercial or institutional building operators can consider benchmarking as a local starting point for establishing an energy baseline; participants need owner consent and the City describes reports/support and retrofit incentive information. Check current BERA eligibility and eligible measures before representing building controls as rebate-eligible. A BAS proposal should inventory existing controls and define measurement, operator responsibility and manual fallback; no particular savings are promised.

**Limitations.** BERA material is dated and program terms/availability can change; confirm current intake and eligibility. Edmonton's video-surveillance directive found in this research governs City public-area operations, not private building operators, so it is not presented as a private-sector rule.

## Toronto

**Supported facts.** Toronto provides a building-permit application guide specifically for stand-alone alterations/replacements of life-safety systems, including fire alarms and electromagnetic locking devices; where other construction is proposed, those alterations may be included in the broader building-permit application. [Fire/Security Upgrades](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/building-construction/building-permit/before-you-apply-for-a-building-permit/building-permit-application-guides/guides-for-other-buildings/firesecurity-upgrades/). Toronto’s security-camera page gives privacy best practices for private residential properties, including limiting capture to one's property and considering notice and retention; it expressly excludes public spaces and private commercial, industrial and retail enterprise spaces from its guidance. [Using a Security Camera on Your Property](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/public-notices-bylaws/bylaw-enforcement/security-cameras/). The City publishes technology companion guidance on building automation systems as part of its Net Zero Building Retrofit Guides for building owners. [Net Zero Building Retrofit Guides: Building Automation Systems](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/87bd-P3.12-Building-Automation-Systems.pdf).

**Local planning paragraph — access/life safety.** For work changing electromagnetic locks or fire-alarm systems, ask whether the project is a stand-alone life-safety upgrade or part of wider construction, and prepare scaled floor plans, system layouts and alteration details described in the City's guide. This is a useful early scoping step, not a blanket permit assertion for ordinary access readers or like-for-like replacements; confirm applicability with Toronto Building for the actual scope.

**Local planning paragraph — CCTV/automation.** For a residential camera enquiry, the City’s guide supports discussing camera framing, neighbouring privacy, signage and retention. Do not apply that residential guidance to commercial or shared-building operations: the page expressly excludes those settings. For BAS planning, the City's guide can support an equipment inventory and retrofit discussion, but it is technical guidance, not evidence of a grant, guaranteed energy savings, or municipal approval.

**Limitations.** Toronto’s camera guidance is expressly limited to private residential property and does not settle obligations for commercial buildings or other applicable privacy law. The Fire/Security Upgrades guide is about life-safety alterations; it does not say all electronic access work requires a permit. No alarm registration/false-alarm source was relied on here.

## Vancouver

**Supported facts.** Vancouver’s false-security-alarm page describes warnings after two false alarms in 12 months and suspension after three or more, with permit reinstatement information. [False security alarms](https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/false-alarms.aspx). City Fire/Building bulletin 2020-009-BU discusses electromagnetic locks within means of egress under the 2019 Vancouver Building By-law, including design conditions and egress-system delays. [Use of electromagnetic locks within means of egress](https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/2020-009-use-of-electromagnetic-locks-within-means-of-egress.pdf). The City’s non-residential gates/doors bulletin explains that some security gates/doors can be permitted under specified conditions and stresses that egress must remain available and unobstructed. [Security Gates and Doors for Non-Residential Floor Areas](https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/bulletin-2024-003-bu-fi-security-gates-doors-nonresidential-floor-areas.pdf). Vancouver publishes current energy requirements/checklists and states applicable current bylaw provisions continue to apply to projects in stream. [Energy requirements, forms, and checklists for all buildings](https://vancouver.ca/home-property-development/large-building-energy-requirements-forms-checklists.aspx).

**Local planning paragraph — alarm.** For monitored security alarms in Vancouver, confirm permit status and keep keyholder/contact details current; the City describes permit suspension as a possible consequence of repeat false alarms. Include a maintenance and false-alarm reduction conversation in the system handover, without promising police response or assuming every alarm event is treated identically.

**Local planning paragraph — doors/automation.** For access-control or security-gate scope, document occupancy type, number and roles of exits, hardware, release logic and proposed delays before design. Vancouver's bulletins address specific means-of-egress conditions and do not authorize every gate or electronic lock. For building controls linked to an energy retrofit, consult the City's applicable building energy checklists and confirm the current requirements for the project; do not claim incentives or that a minor automation change alone triggers a specific approval.

**Limitations.** Vancouver's cited CCTV policy is for City-owned/operated systems and City premises, not a general private-property surveillance rule. The energy page is a requirements/checklist source, not an automation grant. Access bulletins cite building-code contexts; confirm the currently applicable edition and project-specific interpretation with the City.

## Iqaluit

**Supported facts.** Iqaluit's Emergency Services section publishes an “Alarm Contact Information - Annual Revision Form.” [Alarm Contact Information - Annual Revision Form](https://iqaluit.ca/content/alarm-contact-information-annual-revision-form). The actual form asks for building/account and multiple contact details, says the list must be completed for new, permanent or temporary contact changes and updated annually, and explains that dispatch will notify listed persons under the client's alarm-monitoring contract; responsibility for accurate contacts remains with the customer. [Alarm Contact Information form (PDF)](https://iqaluit.ca/sites/default/files/alarm_contact_information_annual_revision_form_-_eng.pdf). The City's Forms and Permits page also lists that form under Emergency Services. [Forms and Permits](https://iqaluit.ca/residents/permits-licences). Iqaluit publishes Building By-Law #710. [Building By-Law #710](https://iqaluit.ca/content/building-law-710).

**Local planning paragraph — alarm/monitoring.** For an alarm-monitoring project in Iqaluit, include the City's annual contact revision in the owner handover plan: confirm who receives notifications, maintain multiple accurate contacts and identify the person responsible for updating changes. The form describes dispatching notifications to contacts under the client's monitoring contract; it does not establish police dispatch, response-time commitments, alarm-system approval, or a City requirement to install a particular system.

**Local planning paragraph — building controls/access.** For a proposed door, exit or automation alteration, use Building By-Law #710 as a starting point and ask the City about the specific scope and current applicable requirements. Record existing equipment, door egress function, interfaces and owner/operator responsibilities before specifying devices. No Iqaluit municipal source establishing a building-automation incentive, private CCTV rule, or universal permit treatment for electronic access alterations was verified here, so avoid asserting one.

**Limitations.** The alarm form is evidence of a contact-maintenance process, not an alarm registration or false-alarm penalty regime. The publicly indexed building by-law page did not provide enough accessible detail in this research to support a claim about a particular device or permit. A 2005 consultant-prepared Iqaluit energy-standard business case was located but is historical analysis, not evidence of a current City program, so it is not used to suggest present incentives.
