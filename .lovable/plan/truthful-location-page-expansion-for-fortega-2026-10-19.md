# Truthful location-page expansion for Fortega

## Goal and safeguards
Recover opportunities in smaller communities through useful, accurate pages—not automatically restore every service × city combination.

- Protect customer identities and security-sensitive information.
- Never invent local offices, partners, credentials, response times, installations or service availability.
- Use confirmed Fortega facts and reliable public sources; leave unknown details out or clearly say they require assessment.
- A shared layout is acceptable. Each separate page must answer a meaningful visitor need.
- No guarantee of indexing, rankings or recovered leads.

## Step-by-step work

### 1. Review the existing and former pages
Inventory the city, province and service-and-city pages, including deleted addresses. Identify repeated claims, unsupported local promises and useful material worth retaining. Use available historical search data to identify previously visible pages; keep your reported leads separate from measured page attribution.

**Result:** a list of candidate pages and claims that need correction—not a bulk restoration.

### 2. Establish the confirmed service-delivery explanation
Describe remote assessments using drawings, layouts, building types and project location. Explain that some installations require travelling technicians and some can involve local partners, without assigning either arrangement to an unconfirmed town. State that availability, installation arrangements and costs are confirmed after assessment.

**Result:** accurate wording that can be used consistently throughout the site.

### 3. Research the first three communities
Start with Dawson City, Yukon; Chetwynd, British Columbia; and Goose Bay, Newfoundland and Labrador. Confirm the appropriate community name and former URL before drafting. Research relevant public facts using official provincial, municipal, regulatory and other reliable sources. Include only facts that help someone plan a security project; do not infer property-level conditions from regional information.

**Result:** a short source-backed brief for each community, with unknowns explicitly identified.

### 4. Choose which pages deserve separate addresses
Prepare one general city-page draft for each pilot community. Evaluate separate service-and-city pages individually: create one only when it can answer a distinct service question with substantial useful detail. Keep provincial guidance on province pages and link to it rather than repeating it in full everywhere.

**Result:** a proposed page list, with an explanation for each page kept separate or consolidated. All eight services will not be assumed available everywhere.

### 5. Draft genuinely useful content
Use relevant assessment checklists, conditional equipment choices, installation and maintenance considerations, sourced provincial guidance and practical questions and answers. Distinguish verified local facts, general recommendations and details to confirm after assessment. Use clearly labelled illustrative diagrams only where they help; never present them as customer installations.

**Result:** three complete city-page drafts and any justified service-page drafts for review before restoration.

### 6. Restore only the reviewed pilot pages
After you approve the page list and wording, restore the selected original addresses where appropriate. Update their page titles, descriptions, links and sitemap entries using the site's existing approach. Remove the deleted-page response only for approved addresses; other removed pages stay deleted. Preserve your chosen site-wide wording and mirror final titles and descriptions in existing social tags without unrelated changes.

**Result:** working pilot pages in the preview. Publishing remains a separate action requiring your instruction.

### 7. Verify before publishing
Check every pilot page for accurate claims, meaningful differences, working contact links, clear headings and readable presentation. Verify page addresses, titles, descriptions, canonical links, structured data and sitemap inclusion. Check that unapproved deleted pages remain deleted and never-existing addresses remain unavailable. Confirm the enquiry flow works; report any part that cannot be verified.

**Result:** a verification summary with any outstanding issues clearly named.

### 8. Publish and measure before expanding
When you authorize publishing, verify the live pages and update the sitemap submission through an accessible search-service connection. Sitemap submission is discovery, not guaranteed indexing. Compare page-level search visibility and qualified enquiries over an initial four-to-six-week observation period; allow longer if activity is sparse. Use enquiry counts and non-identifying town/service information—no customer details. Google Analytics reporting remains dependent on accessible reports or an export; do not claim it is available without checking.

**Result:** an evidence-based recommendation for the next batch, rather than an automatic return to roughly 1,760 pages.

## Technical implementation
- Retain the existing TanStack location, province and service-area routes and metadata mechanisms.
- Separate city-page eligibility from service-and-city eligibility so restoring a town does not automatically publish all eight combinations.
- Keep approved page lists, removed-URL handling, internal links and sitemap generation consistent, with concrete tests for restored, still-deleted and invalid addresses.
- Record source references alongside researched content for future review.
- No database, authentication, email or unrelated site changes are included.
